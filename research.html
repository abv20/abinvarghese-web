Research

My work moves between three connected areas — device-level materials work, the modelling that explains what those devices do at scale, and the earlier body of work in 2D-material optoelectronics that the device work grew out of.

In-memory & neuromorphic computing

Conventional computing keeps memory and processing in separate places, and moving data between them is a major bottleneck for AI workloads. Memristors — devices whose resistance can be set and read electrically — offer a way to compute where the data lives. My work develops the oxide materials (WO₃, HfO₂) and device architectures behind these resistive-switching elements, and studies how defects such as oxygen vacancies can be controlled to make switching more robust and repeatable.

This includes recent work on oxygen-vacancy electrode reservoirs for stable WO₃ memory devices, and on polarization-dependent switching in undoped orthorhombic HfO₂ films — both aimed at devices that hold their state reliably enough for real neuromorphic hardware.

Device physics & modelling

Good devices need models that explain why they behave the way they do — not just data sheets. I work on simulation and modelling of vertical memristor structures, including the impact of geometric factors like lateral electrode offset on switching behaviour, using device simulation frameworks such as those presented at IEEE SISPAD. This modelling connects materials-level choices to the array-level behaviour that matters for neuromorphic circuits.

2D materials & strain-tunable optoelectronics

Earlier in my career, my PhD and postdoctoral work focused on van der Waals heterostructures built from transition metal dichalcogenides (MoS₂, WSe₂, ReS₂, SnSe₂) and their optoelectronic properties — photodetectors, phototransistors, and p–n heterojunctions with electrically tunable responsivity. A parallel thread used piezoelectric thin films to apply controlled strain to 2D semiconductors, giving an electrical handle on their electronic structure, supported by first-principles DFT modelling to explain the observed effects.
