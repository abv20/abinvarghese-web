Research

My work moves between device-level materials development, modelling to predict and analyse device/material behaviours, and evaluating the performance parameters of memristive devices - with the aim to utilise these devices for computing applications. My broad areas of interest are:

In-memory & neuromorphic computing

Conventional computing keeps memory and processing in separate places, and moving data between them is a major bottleneck for AI workloads. Memristors — devices whose resistance/conductance states can be programmed — offer a way to compute where the data lives. My work develops the oxide materials (WO₃, HfO₂) and device architectures behind these resistive-switching elements, and studies how defects such as oxygen vacancies can be controlled to make switching more robust and repeatable.

Device physics & modelling

Reliable devices need physics-based models that explain the observed behaviour. Currently, I use the Ginestra tool from Applied Materials to model memristive device architectures. This modelling connects materials-level choices to the array-level behaviour that are important for neuromorphic implementations.

2D materials & heterostructures

Earlier in my career, my PhD and early part of my postdoctoral work focused on van der Waals heterostructures built from transition metal dichalcogenides (MoS₂, WSe₂, ReS₂, SnSe₂) and their optoelectronic properties — photodetectors, phototransistors, and p–n heterojunctions with electrically tunable responsivity. I also explored heterostructure-based integration of different 2D materials and 2D materials-oxide systems.
